## 1. Key inputs, where they come from, import dependence, margin effects and localisation

### Takeaway
LIL's bill of materials splits into (a) mostly domestic "conventional" parts (resins, moulded parts, bulbs, packaging, about 90% domestic) and (b) LED electronics, which are still heavily imported: **LED modules** ("primarily imported and will continue to be imported"), **connectors** (about 24% localised), **driver ICs** and **optical lens components**. SMT assembly is 100% in-house at the Bawal electronics plant, and bare-PCB localisation stands at 40-70% depending on the source and date. Imports are invoiced mainly in **USD** (about ₹302 crore of FX payables at 31 Mar 2026), then JPY (Stanley). Management treats localisation as a 70-90 bps margin lever and calls out currency, freight (Red Sea) and tariff exposure. I found **no public source naming specific LED-chip makers** (Nichia, ams OSRAM, Lumileds, Seoul Semi) as LIL suppliers. The only named resin is Covestro's Makrolon LED2245 polycarbonate.

### Cited Findings
**LED electronics: what is imported and what is localised**
- CFO Ravi Teltia (May 2025): "LED typically has 4 items. One is the LED module which is primarily being imported and will continue to be imported in the coming years also. The second is a Bare PCB… some PCBs… already localized… The third… SMT… we already have our SMT plant, so it's more of a localized. And the last… connectors are primarily now being imported." — [Q4 FY25 transcript, p.11](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_Q4FY25Transcript.pdf)
- Q1 FY27 call (Aug 2026): SMT 100% localised; bare PCBs 40-50% localised (target 70-80% in 2-3 years); connectors about 24% (target 40-50%); localisation expected to add 70-90 bps to margins in 2-3 years. — [Yahoo Finance call summary, 14 Aug 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lumax-industries-ltd-bom-517206-010455714.html)
- AR FY26: "By achieving 100% localisation in SMT assembly and 60-70% in PCB bare boards… While overall localisation currently stands at 30 to 35%, the Company's aggressive roadmap targets 50 to 60% localisation of electronic parts… insulates… from foreign exchange fluctuations." Also: "Localisation of PCB bare boards reached 60-70% and connectors reached 35-40%, reducing dependence on imports and providing insulation from currency and freight cost volatility." — [AR FY26, pp.21, 25](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)
- **Conflict:** AR FY26 says connectors are 35-40% localised and bare PCBs 60-70% (PCBs "70%" in the ESG table and MD&A). The Q1 FY27 call (Aug 2026) says connectors about 24% and bare PCBs 40-50%. The definitions or bases probably differ, so treat both as approximate. — [AR FY26](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lumax-industries-ltd-bom-517206-010455714.html)
- AR FY26 MD&A (SWOT): "The advanced LED module supply chain, particularly driver ICs and optical lens components, remains significantly import-dependent, creating exposure to tariff volatility and supply-chain disruptions." - AR FY26 risk table: "LED Module Import Dependency: Despite 30-35% overall localization, significant LED driver module imports create exposure to tariff escalation and currency depreciation." LED module localisation is the active next step. **Dual-sourcing from Stanley's global supply network** provides supply continuity and competitive pricing." — [AR FY26, MD&A risk table](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)
- AR FY26 ESG scorecard: "Supplier Code of Conduct enforced. PCB localisation at 70%. **~90% of conventional components domestically sourced**." — [AR FY26, p.35](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)

### Inferences
- *Inference:* LED packages and modules are most likely sourced through Stanley's global network (the AR's "dual-sourcing from Stanley's global supply network") and invoiced in USD, since USD payables are about 12x JPY payables. Currency alone does not reveal country of origin: Japan, China, Taiwan, Korea or Europe are all possible.
- *Inference:* BRSR "within India" (84.5%) and the forex outgo (₹999 crore, about 38% of materials cost) do not reconcile. Outgo includes imported capex (moulds, SMT and moulding machines; FY26 capex was about ₹408 crore) and royalty (₹58 crore). "Within India" may also count imported parts bought from Indian distributors. A demo should say "roughly 15-35% of material spend is import-exposed", not a single precise figure.
- *Inference:* The likely resin set is PC (lenses and light guides, e.g., Covestro Makrolon), PMMA (rear-lamp lenses), PBT/PC-ABS/PP (housings) and BMC (reflectors), plus vacuum-metallizing aluminium. Only the Makrolon grade is documented.

### Gaps
- No primary source names LIL's LED chip/package suppliers (Nichia, ams OSRAM, Lumileds, Seoul Semiconductor, Stanley's own LEDs). Only co-attendance at a 2026 industry event was found ([Driving Vision News India agenda 2026](https://www.drivingvisionnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/India-Agenda-2026-V6.pdf)), which is not evidence of supply.
- No named suppliers for resins other than Covestro (SABIC, Mitsubishi, LG Chem, Röhm), bulbs (Philips/Lumileds, Osram), metallizing materials or wiring.
- No total import value (CIF) or import-share-of-materials figure is disclosed in AR FY26.
- The MNCL claim that LIL "acquired LATL's PCB unit" is unverified. The investor presentation says the Bawal electronics facility started commercial production in 2022 as LIL's own plant, and an earlier electronics facility at Manesar dates from 2019.

## 2. Logistics: delivery to OEMs, inter-plant flows, warehouses, exports and ports

### Takeaway
LIL runs 11 plants in 6 states, sited next to OEM clusters (some literally in OEM vendor parks at Sanand), so outbound flow is short-haul road delivery against OEM daily schedules received by **EDI**. Inbound domestic suppliers ship against SAP-MRP schedules through the **LSETU supplier portal** with mandatory ASNs. Imported electronics come in by sea; JNPT/Nhava Sheva is evidenced by customs records and US shipment data. The Bawal electronics plant supplies PCBs to the other lamp plants. Exports are tiny (0.62% of turnover). I found no named 3PL or logistics partner apart from one freight forwarder on a US export bill of lading.

Tamil Nadu (Sriperumbudur) is SL Lumax, an associate. — [AR FY26, p.24](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)

### Inferences
- *Inference:* The outbound model is JIT/JIS-style supply against OEM daily schedules (EDI), with short road hauls from co-located plants: Sanand plants in the Sanand vendor park (Tata Motors/Ford-now-Tata Sanand cluster and Suzuki/Maruti Gujarat); Chakan and Chinchwad for Tata, M&M, SAVW and Bajaj; Dharuhera and Bawal for Maruti (Manesar/Gurugram/Kharkhoda), Hero and Honda; Pantnagar and Haridwar for Tata Motors Pantnagar, Hero Haridwar and Bajaj Pantnagar; Bengaluru/Bidadi next to Toyota Kirloskar (Bidadi). No source states "milk run" or "JIT" explicitly.
- *Inference:* The main inter-plant flow a demo can model is **Bawal Electronics → all lamp plants (PCBAs/LED boards)**, plus moulds and tools from tool rooms (Dharuhera, Bengaluru) and LATL flows (packing material, spares, semi-finished goods).
- *Inference:* Imported LED/electronic parts most likely enter through JNPT (Nhava Sheva) and possibly Chennai or Mundra, then move by road or rail to Bawal and the other plants. Only JNPT is evidenced.

### Gaps
- No named 3PL, transporter, milk-run operator or warehouse operator (no TCI, Mahindra Logistics, DHL etc.). No warehouse locations disclosed.
- No disclosure of logistics or freight cost as a % of sales.
- No explicit statement of JIT, JIS, milk runs or OEM vendor-park sequencing.

## 3. IT and digital systems: ERP, planning, MES, SCADA/HMI, PLM/CAD, optical tools, quality, Industry 4.0, IT leadership

### Takeaway
LIL runs **SAP S/4HANA under RISE with SAP**, hosted in Google's data centre and managed by SAP (migrated in FY24 from on-prem SAP). It is used for finance, procurement, inventory, production planning (including MRP for domestic and, since FY26, imported material) and quality management across all plants, including new Sanand capacity. Around it sit **SAP Analytics Cloud**, a supplier portal (**LSETU**, with ASNs), **EDI** with OEMs, an **SAP waste portal**, **Google Workspace Enterprise with Gemini**, a **Contract Labour Management System**, digital attendance, and **MES** at the Bawal SMT plant (vendor undisclosed). Engineering runs **Dassault 3DEXPERIENCE** (PLM, implemented by SOLIZE Partners India, announced Jun 2026) and **Autodesk Moldflow**. IT leadership sits at group level: **Namrita Mahindro, Chief Digital & Information Officer, Lumax World** (appointed about Jul 2026). A group posting (Sep 2026) seeks a Head of Digital Manufacturing CoE across "42 manufacturing plants" with SAP S/4HANA PP/QM, MES/MOM, OPC-UA/MQTT and historians. I found no public LIL job post naming specific PLC/SCADA brands.

### Cited Findings
**ERP: SAP S/4HANA RISE**

## 4. People and shifts: shift pattern, contract workforce, unions and labour issues

### Takeaway
LIL is contract-heavy: at 31 Mar 2026 it had **5,084 "other than permanent" workers vs 1,576 permanent workers** (about 76% of the 6,660 workers) plus 1,660 permanent employees. It is only lightly unionised (22% of permanent workers in a union in FY26, down from 26.8%). There are night shifts (a "Women in Night Shift" programme), but no source states the exact shift pattern. I found no reported strikes or labour disputes at LIL's Indian plants.

### Cited Findings
- Workforce at 31 Mar 2026: permanent employees 1,660 (118 women); permanent workers 1,576 (130 women); **other-than-permanent workers 5,084 (1,400 women, 27.5%)**; total workers 6,660. — [AR FY26 BRSR, Section A Q20](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)

## 5. Sustainability items relevant to operations (energy, solar, water)

LIL used about **427 TJ of energy in FY26 (up 22% year on year)**, almost all of it electricity (about 113 GWh).
