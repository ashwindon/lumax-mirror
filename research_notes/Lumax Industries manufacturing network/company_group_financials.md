## 1. Group structure, promoters, management, Stanley Electric and subsidiaries/JVs

### Takeaway
LIL is a 50:50 promoter company. The Indian D.K. Jain family holds 37.5% and Japan's Stanley Electric group holds 37.5%; Stanley has been technical collaborator since 1984 and an equity partner since 1994. LIL has one wholly owned subsidiary (Lumax Industries Czech s.r.o., an engineering centre in Ostrava) and one associate (SL Lumax Ltd, 21.28%, Chennai, which mainly supplies Hyundai). It also has a design centre in Taiwan. In May 2026 the next generation took over formally: Deepak Jain became Chairman, Anmol Jain became MD, and founder-generation D.K. Jain is Chairman Emeritus.

### Cited Findings

**Group history (D.K. Jain / Lumax Group)**
- The group was founded in 1945 by the late S.C. Jain as a trading firm. An automotive lighting manufacturing unit followed in 1955, and the business later diversified into filters and rear-view mirrors — [ICRA rationale, 27 Apr 2026 (URL ID inferred, see note)](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=142650)
- Group timeline: 'Globe Auto Industries' founded as a trading partnership in 1945; dedicated automotive lighting units in Delhi-NCR and Maharashtra between 1955 and 1975; Lumax Industries Pvt Ltd established from Globe Auto Industries; Lumax Auto Electricals set up to make 2W lighting at Bhosari, Maharashtra (later renamed Lumax Auto Technologies); JV with SL Corporation, Korea in 1997; LATL listed between 2005 and 2015; LATL JVs with Cornaglia (Italy), Mannoh (Japan), FAE (Spain), Ituran (Israel), Yokowo (Japan) and Alps Alpine (Japan) — [Q2 FY27 investor presentation, 26 Oct 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/LUMAXIND_26102026201707_LILIPQ2FY2027.pdf)
- LIL milestones: incorporated as a private limited company in 1981 (CIN L74899DL1981PLC012804); technical agreement with Stanley for lighting equipment in 1984; listed on the stock exchanges in 1985; Stanley equity participation in 1994; SL Lumax JV in 1997 — [Q2 FY27 IP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/LUMAXIND_26102026201707_LILIPQ2FY2027.pdf). **Conflict:** ICRA says LIL "went public in 1984" — [ICRA Apr 2026](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=142650)
- Group scale (FY26, company figures): 8 decades, 41 manufacturing facilities in 8 states, 10 partnerships, 7 design/R&D/engineering and tech centres, 15,000+ employees, and about INR 12,000 Cr "Lumax brand revenue". That revenue figure includes SL Lumax, an associate. - **LATL (sister company, context only):** "diversified automotive systems" covering advanced plastics and interiors, structures and control systems, mechatronics, alternate fuels (CNG) and aftermarket. **Nature of the Stanley Electric collaboration**
- The company describes the relationship as "JV for lighting, HVAC panels and other electronics". The alliance has been crucial in the design and manufacture of advanced lighting and "facilitated the production of Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs)" for LED lamps for Indian customers. In 2020 the partnership was extended to electronics and HVAC — [Q2 FY27 IP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/LUMAXIND_26102026201707_LILIPQ2FY2027.pdf)
- The Bawal electronics facility was "developed as part of the extended Stanley Electric JV". The partnership has moved from PCB production to developing HVAC control panels, which are localised and made at Bawal. No separate JV legal entity appears among LIL's subsidiaries or associates — [AR 2025-26](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)
- Stanley's support in practice: two senior Stanley designers are stationed at LIL; LIL engineers are trained at Stanley in Japan; no technology was "imported" in FY24 to FY26 under the formal disclosure — [AR 2025-26, Annexure G](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_integrated_annual_report_2025_26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The shareholder register counts Stanley Electric Co. and Thai Stanley among "promoters", so Stanley is a co-promoter rather than a financial investor. Any product demo for leadership should treat both the Jain family (Chairman/MD) and the Stanley nominees (Senior ED, ED) as decision-makers.
- The Stanley economics are material. Royalty, technical fees and support fees came to about INR 74 Cr in FY26, roughly 18% of FY26 EBITDA (INR 412 Cr). Royalty rose about 47% while revenue rose 23%, which suggests royalty is linked to sales of Stanley-licensed or LED products. That link is inferred, not stated.
- Deepak Jain and Anmol Jain are understood to be D.K. Jain's sons, and Anmol Jain is understood to also lead LATL. The annual report lists D.K. Jain only as a "relative of KMP", so the exact family and LATL roles were not verified in this research.

### Gaps
- Named plant heads are not public. The annual report mentions only generic "Plant Head" roles in safety patrols.
- No current document gives the formal terms of the Stanley technical assistance agreement (royalty rate, term).
- No standalone financials of Lumax Industries Czech s.r.o. were extracted. The annual report text was truncated at that point.

## 2. Financials: FY23 to FY26, Q1 and Q2 FY27, mix, LED, exports, capex, debt and ratings

### Takeaway
Revenue grew from INR 2,320 Cr (FY23) to INR 4,184 Cr (FY26), a 21% three-year CAGR. EBITDA was INR 412 Cr in FY26 at a 9.8% margin, and consolidated PAT was INR 172.5 Cr. H1 FY27 revenue rose 33% to INR 2,565 Cr, but EBITDA margin fell to 8.7% (Q2: 8.2%) on input-cost and geopolitical supply-chain pressure, below the FY27 guidance of 10.5 to 11%. LED is 61 to 63% of revenue and 88% of the order book. Passenger vehicles (PV) are about 63 to 65% of revenue, and exports are negligible (0.62%). ICRA upgraded LIL to AA-(Stable)/A1+ in April 2026. Debt is about INR 800 to 890 Cr, mostly short-term.

## 4. Strategy and news, 2024 to 2026: plants, technology, customers, EV, order book and management commentary

### Takeaway
LIL is executing an "LED + electronics + localisation" strategy. Steps so far:
- New plants at Chakan III (Phase 1 in FY24, Phase 2 in FY26) and Sanand II (Q1 FY26), plus electronics and PCB at Bawal.
- A greenfield Bengaluru plant (INR 140 Cr) for Maruti and Toyota due in Q4 FY27.
- First ADB headlamp in India (Maruti e Vitara).
- A single-source HMSI Activa/Shine LED win.
- HVAC panels as a second growth engine.

Targets are LED share of 65 to 70%, early-teens EBITDA margin and more than INR 9,000 Cr revenue by FY31. Key headwinds: LED electronics import dependence (only 30 to 35% localised), input-cost and geopolitical shocks, customer concentration and working-capital strain.

### Inferences
- The operational story for a demo: 11 owned plants clustered near OEM hubs (NCR/Haryana, Pune, Sanand, Uttarakhand, Bengaluru) running at 70 to 80% utilisation, simultaneous new-plant ramp-ups (Chakan III Phase 2, Sanand II, and the Bengaluru greenfield in FY27), a dollar-exposed electronics bill of materials, roughly one-quarter cost pass-through lag, a contract-heavy workforce, and a mould/tooling business that lumps into H2. Together these suggest S&OP, input-cost recovery tracking, plant ramp-up visibility and working-capital control as natural use cases. This is an inference, not a company statement.
- **Bengaluru naming is ambiguous.** The AR calls the INR 140 Cr Bengaluru plant a "new greenfield" and LIL's "strategic entry into India's southern automotive manufacturing hub", yet LIL has operated a Bidadi (Bengaluru) plant since 2011. The new facility is probably a second Bengaluru site, but the documents read do not confirm this.

## 5. Market position in Indian automotive lighting vs Uno Minda, Fiem, Varroc/Plastic Omnium, Marelli, Hella

### Takeaway
LIL describes itself as the leader in Indian automotive lighting. ICRA, the most authoritative current source (Apr 2026), calls it the leading PV lighting supplier and third-largest in 2W. Its named competitors are Uno Minda (PV & 2W), Fiem (2W), India Japan Lighting (PV) and Marelli Motherson (PV). Market-share figures of about 50% (and a company claim of 60%) are dated, from 2018 to 2021, and some include SL Lumax and LATL.

### Cited Findings
- LIL is "the leading supplier of lighting solutions for the PV segment and the third largest in the 2W segment". Competitors are "Uno Minda Limited (PVs and 2Ws), FIEM Industries Limited (2Ws), India Japan Lighting (PVs) and Marelli Motherson Automotive Lighting India Private Limited (PVs)". The PV lighting market is "highly competitive, which renders the margins... to remain at moderate levels" — [ICRA, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=142650)
- An older ICRA rationale (date not confirmed) said LIL, together with SL Lumax and LATL, has "close to 50% market share in the domestic automotive lighting market". It placed LIL third in 2W after FIEM and Minda (including Rinder) (secondary, via search snippet) — [ICRA rationale (older)](https://www.icra.in/Rating/ShowRationalReportFilePdfViewer/92434)
- HDFC Securities' initiation (12 Apr 2021) gives about 50% market share in domestic automobile lighting (secondary snippet) — [HDFC Securities, Apr 2021](https://www.hdfcsec.com/hsl.research.pdf/Initiating%20Coverage%20-%20Lumax%20Industries_12%20Apr%202021.pdf)
- A "60% market share" claim attributed to the company profile (date unknown; secondary) — [InvestYadnya](https://investyadnya.in/stock-o-meter/lumax-industries)
- Company self-description: "Leader in automotive lighting: end-to-end exterior lighting, interior cabin lighting and new-technology lighting (AFS, ADB, Matrix)"; "Market Leadership in automotive Lighting" — [Q2 FY27 IP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/LUMAXIND_26102026201707_LILIPQ2FY2027.pdf); [Q2 FY26 IP](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LILIP08112025.pdf)
- A paid market-research listing ranks Lumax first among India lighting players, followed by Varroc Group, HELLA and Uno Minda. This is weak evidence (marketing page) — [GII / Mordor listing](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/moi2063267-india-automotive-lighting-market-share-analysis.html)
- Wallet-share proxies (May 2025, management): about 27 to 30% of Maruti's lighting purchases; about 50% of M&M's lighting; about 60% of HMSI's — [Q4 FY25 transcript](https://www.lumaxworld.in/lumaxindustries/pdf/LIL_Q4FY25Transcript.pdf)
- Sister-company context: SL Lumax, the Hyundai-focused associate, had FY26 revenue of INR 2,933 Cr. This is why "Lumax brand" share claims can be much larger than LIL's own share — [Q2 FY27 IP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/LUMAXIND_26102026201707_LILIPQ2FY2027.pdf)

### Inferences
- The roughly 50% "market share" figures cover the group perimeter (LIL plus SL Lumax plus LATL) and are 5 to 8 years old. They should not be presented as LIL's current standalone share. The defensible current statement is ICRA's: number 1 in PV lighting, number 3 in 2W.
- Varroc Polymers' 4W lighting business (VLS) was sold to Plastic Omnium around 2022. That is background knowledge, not verified in this research. The newer ICRA (2026) list of competitors does not name Varroc, Plastic Omnium or Hella, which suggests they are less relevant in LIL's core Indian OEM accounts.

### Gaps
- No current (2024 to 2026) numeric market share for LIL, Uno Minda lighting, Fiem, Marelli Motherson, India Japan Lighting or Plastic Omnium in India was found from a credible source.
- I found no analyst (broker) report from 2025 to 2026 with a market-share estimate.
